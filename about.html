About

Born 1985, Moscow. Lives and works in Cologne, Germany, since November 2024.

Ira is a video and mixed media artist working with performative and process-based formats. Her practice investigates the body as an analytical field where social norms, institutional expectations, and political regimes intersect with individual bodily experience. Her work focuses on vulnerability, memory, and the post-Soviet transformation of cultural codes, as well as on mythological and ritual structures that continue to shape everyday modes of perception and behavior.

Particular attention is given to posthuman perspectives and body hybridization at the intersection of the organic, the social, and the technological. Ira works with video, performance, and mixed media, using duration, repetition, and collective formats as methods of artistic research. Vulnerability in her projects functions not as an expressive gesture but as an analytical tool for revealing mechanisms of power, discipline, and normalization embedded in bodily and everyday experience.

She regularly works in collective and interdisciplinary contexts. Ira is a co-founder of the self-organized initiatives Experimental Field and ShShSh.

Her artistic method is based on processuality, duration, and the investigation of boundaries between individual experience and collective responsibility.

Statement

Art as a method for examining vulnerability, power, and collective responsibility.

In my artistic practice, I work with video, performance, and process-based formats to investigate how bodies, vulnerability, and memory are embedded in social, political, and cultural structures. I am interested in situations where personal experience becomes a site of pressure, control, and redistributed responsibility.

I approach art as a space for working with unstable states between visibility and disappearance, speech and silence, presence and absence. Duration and processuality play a central role in my work, allowing me to register conditions rather than produce fixed results.

My practice is grounded in working with the body as a carrier of experience and memory, as well as with archetypal and mythological structures emerging through post-Soviet cultural codes, folk rituals, and collective forms of knowledge. I am interested in these structures not as markers of identity but as persistent systems shaping perception, obedience, and survival.

The body in my work functions as an analytical field where external forces such as social norms, institutional expectations, and political regimes intersect with internal responses. Vulnerability is not used as an expressive device but as a method for exposing mechanisms of power, discipline, and normalization embedded in bodily and everyday experience.

I often work in collective and self-organized formats, treating joint action as a way to resist isolation, fragmentation, and the individualization of responsibility. For me, art is not a representation of personal experience but a method for investigating the boundaries between the individual and the collective, between support, interdependence, and the limits of delegating responsibility.
